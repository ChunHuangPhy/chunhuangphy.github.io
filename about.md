    <h2>A physicist working between theory, observation, and computation.</h2>
    <p>I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Physics at Washington University in St. Louis, advised by Prof. Alex Chen. My doctoral work develops physics-motivated models of pulsar surface heating and uses X-ray timing observations from NICER and XMM-Newton to infer neutron-star properties.</p>
    <p>Alongside this work, I develop model-independent methods for neutron-star radius and tidal inference and coordinate the CompactObject open-source collaboration. I enjoy building research infrastructure that makes ambitious analyses reproducible—and mentoring researchers as they turn new ideas into finished papers.</p>

    <h2>A path into astrophysics.</h2>
    <p>I grew up in Jishou, a small mountain city in Hunan Province, China, and I am the first person in my family to attend college. I studied physics at Central China Normal University in Wuhan before moving to St. Louis for my Ph.D. The distance between those places—and the teachers, collaborators, and friends who made the journey possible—continues to shape how I approach science and community.</p>
